Media note

Georgia Film Society is best described as a nonprofit cinematic cultural institution for Georgia and the Southeast, with The Aster Film Festival as its flagship festival.

Approved language

Clear language for public use.

Short description

Georgia Film Society is a nonprofit cinematic cultural institution for Georgia and the Southeast.

Standard description

Georgia Film Society is a nonprofit cinematic cultural institution dedicated to storytelling, artistic excellence, filmmaker support, cultural conversation, education, membership, patronage, and the future of film in Georgia and the Southeast.

Extended description

Georgia Film Society is building a year-round cultural institution for cinema, filmmakers, patrons, students, partners, and audiences across Georgia and the Southeast. Its long-term ecosystem includes curated screenings, salons, education initiatives, filmmaker-centered programming, membership, patronage, partnerships, future-of-storytelling conversations, and The Aster Film Festival as its flagship festival brand.

The Aster language

The Aster is the flagship festival of Georgia Film Society.

Describe The Aster as a prestige discovery platform built around merit, curation, hospitality, and serious cinematic work. It is part of the larger Georgia Film Society ecosystem, not a replacement for the institution.

Language to avoid

Keep the public framing institution-first.

Not a club

Avoid describing GFS as a local film club, hobby group, or casual community meetup.

Not festival-only

The Aster is the flagship festival, but Georgia Film Society is the larger cultural institution.

Not startup language

Avoid hype-first, gimmicky, trend-chasing, or startup-style framing.

Not generic arts copy

Avoid vague “support the arts” language as the primary institutional description.

Media inquiries

Tell the story clearly.

Georgia Film Society is a cinematic cultural institution rooted in storytelling, artistic ambition, hospitality, creative discovery, and serious long-term support for film culture in Georgia and the Southeast.
