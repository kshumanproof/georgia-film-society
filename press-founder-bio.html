Full bio

Approved long-form biography.

+

Kris Shuman is the founder of Georgia Film Society, an emerging cinematic cultural institution dedicated to storytelling, artistic excellence, filmmaker support, cultural conversation, and the future of creative culture in Georgia and the American South.

A Georgia-born writer, producer, filmmaker, and entrepreneur, Shuman created Georgia Film Society from a belief that the state’s rise as a major production center should be matched by stronger cultural institutions — places where filmmakers, audiences, patrons, students, and creative leaders can gather around work that matters.

His vision for Georgia Film Society is rooted in the long view: building an institution rather than a single event, emphasizing prestige over scale, curation over volume, and filmmaker respect over transactional programming. The organization is being developed as a year-round cinematic ecosystem that may eventually include festivals, curated screenings, filmmaker conversations, writer and producer labs, educational initiatives, member salons, hospitality experiences, and future-of-storytelling programming.

As a storyteller, Shuman’s creative interests center on character-driven work, moral complexity, family legacy, regional identity, and the emotional truths that shape people across generations. His scripted projects include What’s Buried Beneath the Pines, a Southern Gothic crime drama set against the Georgia timber landscape.

Through Georgia Film Society, Shuman seeks to help strengthen the creative culture surrounding cinema in Georgia and the South — not as a reaction to existing creative capitals, but as a serious cultural voice in its own right.
