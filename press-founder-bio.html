Standard bio

Kris Shuman is the founder of Georgia Film Society, a nonprofit cinematic cultural institution for Georgia and the Southeast. A screenwriter, producer, and media host from Georgia, Shuman’s work centers storytelling, regional identity, creative ambition, and the role of cinema in public culture.

Through Georgia Film Society, Shuman is advancing an institution designed to support cinema culture beyond a single event. The Society’s ecosystem includes year-round programming, curated screenings, filmmaker-centered conversations, student access, membership, patronage, partnerships, and The Aster Film Festival as its flagship festival.

His goal is to help create a serious, story-first cultural platform where filmmakers, audiences, patrons, students, civic leaders, and creative partners can gather around cinema with intention, hospitality, and long-term purpose.

Leadership focus

Institution-first, story-first, and grounded in long-term cultural purpose.

Cinema Culture

Supporting a home for screenings, conversations, salons, festivals, and year-round cinematic life.

Filmmakers

Creating serious environments where artists, writers, and filmmakers are treated with care and credibility.

Student Access

Developing pathways for students, interns, educators, and future storytellers across Georgia and the Southeast.

Patronage

Inviting members, patrons, sponsors, and civic partners to strengthen long-term cultural infrastructure around film.
